Review of the Paper: "Reinforcement Learning-Genetic Algorithm Framework for Adaptive Parameter Control in Optimizing Robotic Workcell Layouts"

Summary:
The manuscript presents a reinforcement learning-genetic algorithm (RL-GA) framework for optimizing two-dimensional robotic workcell layouts. A tabular Q-learning agent dynamically adjusts mutation rate, crossover rate, local-search rate, and mutation strategy based on discretized population diversity and recent fitness improvement. The approach is evaluated on randomly generated eight-machine layout problems against genetic-algorithm baselines using local-search rates of 10 % and 100 %.
The combination of adaptive parameter control with constrained workcell-layout optimization is relevant to intelligent manufacturing. The manuscript is generally well structured, the algorithmic workflow is illustrated clearly, and the authors openly discuss several limitations concerning reward design, hard-coded states, and real-world feasibility. The provision of source code and research data is also appreciated.
However, the present experimental design does not isolate the effect of reinforcement-learning-based parameter control. The RL-GA applies local search to elite chromosomes, whereas the baseline GA applies it to random crossover offspring. Consequently, both the control policy and the underlying search architecture change simultaneously. Moreover, three runs per problem are insufficient for a robust comparison of stochastic optimization algorithms, and the use of minimum best fitness emphasizes favorable outliers rather than reliable performance. Substantial methodological revision and additional experiments are therefore required before the claimed advantages can be assessed conclusively.
Points to be addressed by the authors:
1. Fairness of the algorithm comparison and attribution of improvements:
a. The most critical issue is that RL-GA and the baseline GA differ in more than adaptive parameter control. Local search is applied to elites in RL-GA but to random crossover offspring in the baseline. This gives RL-GA an additional exploitation mechanism and prevents attribution of the observed improvements specifically to Q-learning.
b. Please conduct an ablation study in which all algorithms use the same local-search target and differ only in parameter control. At minimum, compare: static GA with local search on offspring, static GA with local search on elites, RL-GA with local search on offspring, and RL-GA with local search on elites.
c. The mutation-strategy cycling available only to RL-GA introduces a further difference. A matched static or scheduled multi-strategy baseline is needed to determine whether gains arise from reinforcement learning or simply from access to a richer operator set.Heat-treatment allocation and scale dependence:
2. Statistical validity and number of independent runs:
a. Only three runs are performed per test problem. This is too small for reliable characterization of stochastic GA and RL-GA performance. The experiments should be repeated with a substantially larger number of independent seeds, commonly at least 20–30 runs per instance.
b. Report median, mean, standard deviation or interquartile range, and confidence intervals for solution quality and runtime. Apply an appropriate paired statistical test across matched seeds and include an effect size rather than relying only on percentage differences.
c. ‘Minimum best fitness’ selects the most favorable run and should not be treated as the primary performance metric. Emphasize the distribution and expected performance across runs. The wording ‘robustness’ and claims of consistent improvement should be moderated until statistically supported.
3. Computational-budget fairness and the claimed quality-runtime trade-off:
a. All approaches run for 300 generations, but different local-search rates lead to substantially different numbers of fitness evaluations and computational effort. Equal generation counts therefore do not constitute an equal computational budget.
b. Compare the methods under matched wall-clock time and/or matched numbers of objective-function evaluations, including all local-search evaluations and RL overhead. Convergence should be plotted against both evaluations and time, not only generations.
c. The reward function contains no runtime or computational-cost term. The agent therefore does not actually learn a quality-runtime trade-off; it mainly learns that frequent elite local search improves fitness. Claims that RL-GA acts as a trade-off agent should be revised or supported by a multi-objective/cost-aware reward and corresponding experiments.
4. Baselines and ablation of the adaptive-control components:
a. The 10% and 100% local-search baselines are not sufficient to establish superiority over static hyperparameter tuning. Include a tuned static GA, intermediate fixed local-search rates, and a simple deterministic or self-adaptive schedule.
b. Ablate the individual controlled parameters to quantify the contribution of mutation-rate adaptation, crossover-rate adaptation, local-search-rate adaptation, and mutation-strategy selection. The current results suggest that local search dominates, but this is not demonstrated quantitatively.
c. Please compare against an established adaptive parameter-control or hyper-heuristic baseline where feasible. This would strengthen the novelty claim beyond the specific application of tabular Q-learning.
5. Training, testing, and generalization:
a. Provide complete details on how training and test instances were generated, including machine dimensions, L-shape construction, access-point placement, sequence generation, random seeds, feasibility rates, and instance difficulty. The repository link does not replace a self-contained methodological description.
b. Clarify how the empirical state-bin thresholds were determined and whether any test-instance information influenced their selection. A distinct training, validation, and test protocol is required to avoid data leakage and threshold tuning on the reported test set.
c. Training a single agent sequentially on 15 problems for three epochs may make the learned policy dependent on problem order and initialization. Repeat the complete training procedure with independent seeds and report policy/Q-table stability. Evaluation should cover unseen distributions, different machine counts, workspace sizes, and constraint densities, or the claims should be restricted explicitly to eight-machine instances.
6. Problem formulation and fitness-function validity:
a. The fitness function combines travel distance, span penalties, and a squared-distance accessibility bonus with empirically selected weights. Discuss dimensional consistency, normalization, and sensitivity to α and β. Without normalization, the relative influence of the terms changes with workspace scale and machine count.
b. The accessibility term based on distances to machine corner points requires clearer justification. Demonstrate that it represents operational access clearance and does not reward layouts that remain unreachable or obstructed from the robot position.
c. The manuscript relates reduced Euclidean access-point distance to cycle time, wear, and energy consumption, but does not model robot kinematics, acceleration, collision-free paths, or task duration. These claims should be moderated. At minimum, distinguish geometric surrogate fitness from actual robot cycle time and validate the surrogate on representative layouts if broader manufacturing conclusions are retained.
7. Feasibility and industrial relevance of the layouts:
a. Collision-free machine footprints within a 2D boundary are insufficient to establish a feasible robotic workcell. The current framework omits robot reachability, swept volumes, approach directions, safety distances, maintenance access, material flow, cables, doors, human access, and collision-free motion planning.
b. These omissions are acknowledged, but the abstract and conclusion still refer broadly to constrained engineering optimization and a benchmark for real-life deployment. Please moderate these claims and define the method as a proof of concept for simplified 2D layout instances.
c. If possible, include at least one realistic case study or a post-hoc feasibility assessment using robot reachability and path planning. This would considerably strengthen the relevance to manufacturing practice.
8. Presentation and interpretation of results:
a. The aggregated tables conceal the variability across problems and runs. Present per-instance distributions using box or violin plots, paired fitness differences, and runtime-versus-quality scatter plots or Pareto fronts. Confidence intervals should be shown where applicable.
b. Figure 2 is based on a single sample run and cannot establish a general learned control behavior. Aggregate action frequencies and parameter trajectories across independent runs and test instances, and provide the learned policy or Q-table in an interpretable form.
c. Discuss Problem 9 and other degradations more systematically. Relate performance to measurable instance characteristics such as free-space ratio, geometric irregularity, or constraint density rather than offering only qualitative explanations.
9. Novelty, reproducibility, and editorial corrections:
a. The manuscript states that no RL-GA framework exists for robotic workcell layouts, while also noting that the work is based on an earlier preliminary study by the first author. Clearly distinguish the new scientific contributions from that prior work and from established RL-based GA parameter-control studies.
b. Ensure that the released data and code contain exact configurations, seeds, trained Q-tables, raw results for every run, and scripts reproducing all tables and figures. State the software/hardware environment and whether runtime measurements were controlled for background processes.
c. The manuscript should be proofread carefully. Examples include ‘tasks sequence’, ‘at earlier generations’, ‘best best fitness’, ‘This study is only limited’, inconsistent use of ‘RL-GA Agent’, and the space before the semicolon in the corresponding-author line. The GeeksforGeeks reference should be replaced by an authoritative computational-geometry source.
By addressing these points, the authors can substantially improve the methodological validity, reproducibility, and practical interpretability of the work.
